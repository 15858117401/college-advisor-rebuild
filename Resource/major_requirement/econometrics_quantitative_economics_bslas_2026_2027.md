# Econometrics & Quantitative Economics, BSLAS — Major Requirements

- Catalog year: 2026–2027
- Official source: [Econometrics & Quantitative Economics, BSLAS — Degree Requirements](https://catalog.illinois.edu/undergraduate/las/econometrics-quantitative-economics-bslas/#degreerequirementstext)

## Required Major Courses

### Economics core

The core and the 11-hour general Economics elective group below comprise **at least 30 hours of Economics**. ECON 199, ECON 220, ECON 398, and ECON 399 do not count toward that minimum.

| Course | Hours |

| ECON 102 — Microeconomic Principles | 3 |
| ECON 103 — Macroeconomic Principles | 3 |
| ECON 198 — Economics at Illinois | 1 |
| ECON 202 — Economic Statistics I | 3 |
| ECON 203 — Economic Statistics II | 3 |
| ECON 302 — Inter Microeconomic Theory | 3 |
| ECON 303 — Inter Macroeconomic Theory | 3 |

## Required Preparatory and Supporting Coursework

### Mathematics, Statistics, and Computer Science — 15–16 hours

Complete each row.

| Course or choice | Hours |
| :-- | --: |
| MATH 220 — Calculus **or** MATH 221 — Calculus I | 5 (catalog choice row) |
| MATH 231 — Calculus II | 3 |
| MATH 225 — Introductory Matrix Theory | 2 |
| STAT 385 — Statistics Programming Methods | 3 |
| CS 105 — Intro Computing: Non-Tech **or** CS 101 — Intro Computing: Engrg & Sci **or** CS 125 — Introduction to Computer Science | 3 (catalog choice row) |

The degree table prints 5 hours for the calculus choice, but the [official MATH entries](https://catalog.illinois.edu/courses-of-instruction/math/) specify MATH 220 as 5 hours and MATH 221 as 4. The degree table prints 3 hours for the computing choice, while the [official CS entries](https://catalog.illinois.edu/courses-of-instruction/cs/) specify CS 105 and CS 101 as 3 hours and CS 125 as 4. These alternatives and the degree table's 15–16-hour group total are retained without silently reconciling the difference.

### Supporting coursework — 6 hours

Complete **6 hours outside Economics related to econometrics, quantitative economics, computational economics, or mathematical economics**, including additional coursework in Mathematics, Statistics, or Computer Science.

## Major Electives

### Additional Economics — 11 hours

Complete **11 additional hours at the 300- or 400-level**, excluding ECON 398 and ECON 399. The core and elective minimum also excludes ECON 199 and ECON 220.

### Quantitative Economics — 12 hours

Complete a separate **12 hours of 400-level Economics** from the set of econometrics, quantitative economics, computational economics, and mathematical economics courses. The catalog does not enumerate individual eligible courses. The department's [approved econometric and quantitative electives list](https://economics.illinois.edu/academics/undergraduate-program/majors-minor/econ-course-numbering-overview/econometric) supplies these options:

- ECON 425 — Macroeconomic Policy
- ECON 437 — Game Theory
- ECON 451 — Program Evaluation in Development Economics
- ECON 460 — Financial Economics
- ECON 469 — Economics of Risk
- ECON 472 — Financial Econometrics
- ECON 474 — Econometrics of Policy Evaluation
- ECON 475 — Economic Forecasting (the department’s approved list abbreviates the title as “Econ Forecasting”; full title from the [official ECON entry](https://catalog.illinois.edu/courses-of-instruction/econ/)).
- ECON 491 — Topics in Econometrics: all topics qualify. The approved list specifically includes the following topics, which it also identifies under former ECON 490 credit:
  - International Finance
  - International Macro Forecasting
  - Nonlinear Econometric Models
  - Numerical Methods: Economics
  - Applied Machine Learning in Economics
  - Macroeconomics of Labor Markets
  - Predictive Analytics
  - Macroeconometric Policy
  - Econometrics of Decision-Making
  - Data Analysis: Problem Solving (listed under ECON 491).

The linked list excludes courses outside Economics. Advanced transfer courses require departmental evaluation after articulation as ECON 3-- or ECON 4--.
