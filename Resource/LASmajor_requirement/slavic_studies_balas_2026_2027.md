# Slavic Studies, BALAS — Major Requirements

- Catalog year: 2026–2027
- Official source: [Slavic Studies — required concentration choices](https://catalog.illinois.edu/undergraduate/las/slavic-studies-balas/)

## Required Major Courses

Choose one concentration below and complete its corresponding language and elective requirements. Every concentration requires at least 6 hours of language beyond the second year and 24 hours of literature and culture. Study-abroad courses may replace major requirements with advisor approval.

### Czech Studies: introductory culture — 6 hours

[Official Czech Studies requirements](https://catalog.illinois.edu/undergraduate/las/slavic-studies-balas/czech-studies/#degreerequirementstext).

Complete SLAV 120 — Russian & E Euro Folktales **and** one of:

- REES 201 — Introduction to Eastern Europe
- RUSS 261 — Introduction to Jewish Culture in the Russian Empire
- SLAV 117 — Russ & E Euro Science Fiction

Choose one in consultation with the Undergraduate Advisor:

## Required Preparatory and Supporting Coursework

Complete the language group for the selected concentration. The credit totals below apply to each whole alternative.

### Czech Studies — at least 6 hours

Complete CZCH 484 — Readings in Czech for 6 hours, or equivalent, beyond second-year Czech; alternatively, complete the second-year (200-level) sequence of a second Slavic language, including Russian, in addition to Czech.

### Polish Studies — at least 6 hours

Complete POL 301 — Third-Year Polish I **and** POL 302 — Third-Year Polish II, or equivalent, beyond second-year Polish; alternatively, complete the second-year (200-level) sequence of a second Slavic language, including Russian, in addition to Polish. Course titles are supplied by the [official POL course entries](https://catalog.illinois.edu/courses-of-instruction/pol/).

### Russian Language, Literature, and Culture — at least 6 hours

Complete RUSS 301 — Third Year Russian I **and** RUSS 302 — Third Year Russian II, or equivalent, beyond second-year Russian.

### South Slavic Studies — at least 6 hours

Complete the catalog-listed sequence SCR 301 — Third-Year Serbian/Croatian I **and** SCR 302 — Third-Year Serbian/Croatian II, or equivalent, beyond second-year study; alternatively, complete the second-year (200-level) sequence of a second Slavic language, including Russian, in addition to SCR.

The requirement page uses SCR 301 and SCR 302, but the official 2026–2027 course catalog lists [BCS 301 — Third Year Bosnian-Croatian-Serbian I and BCS 302 — Third Year Bosnian-Croatian-Serbian II](https://catalog.illinois.edu/courses-of-instruction/bcs/) and contains no SCR subject entries. The stated SCR sequence is retained without assuming an equivalency.

### Ukrainian Studies — at least 6 hours

Complete the catalog-listed UKR 301 **and** UKR 302, or equivalent, beyond second-year Ukrainian; alternatively, complete the second-year (200-level) sequence of a second Slavic language, including Russian, in addition to Ukrainian.

The requirement page does not supply titles for UKR 301 or UKR 302, and neither code appears in the official [2026–2027 UKR course entries](https://catalog.illinois.edu/courses-of-instruction/ukr/). No title or replacement sequence is inferred.

## Major Electives

### Czech Studies — 18 hours beyond introductory culture

- Literature surveys: 6 hours, selected with the Undergraduate Advisor from the Slavic Department's maintained list.
- Upper-level literature and culture: 12 hours at the 300 or 400 level, selected with the Undergraduate Advisor from the department's maintained list.

Up to 9 hours of the literature requirement, including no more than 6 upper-level hours, may be replaced by same-level courses in other departments on the Czech region's history, culture, and society, with Undergraduate Advisor approval.

### Polish Studies — 18 hours beyond introductory culture

- Literature surveys: 6 hours, selected with the Undergraduate Advisor from the Slavic Department's maintained list.
- Upper-level literature and culture: 12 hours at the 300 or 400 level, selected with the Undergraduate Advisor from the department's maintained list.

POL 401 — Fourth-Year Polish I and POL 402 — Fourth-Year Polish II may count toward any literature-and-culture category ([official course titles](https://catalog.illinois.edu/courses-of-instruction/pol/)). Up to 9 hours of the literature requirement, including no more than 6 upper-level hours, may be replaced by same-level courses in other departments on Poland's or the region's history, culture, and society, with Undergraduate Advisor approval.

### Russian Language, Literature, and Culture — 18 hours beyond introductory culture

Literature surveys: complete two 200-level literature or cinema courses, totaling 6 hours, selected with the Undergraduate Advisor from the Slavic Department's maintained list.

Author courses: select 6 hours from:

- RUSS 320 — Major Writers
- RUSS 322 — Dostoevsky
- RUSS 323 — Tolstoy
- RUSS 335 — Nabokov

Additional literature and culture: complete two 400-level courses, totaling 6 hours, selected with the Undergraduate Advisor from the department's maintained list.

RUSS 401 — Fourth Year Russian I and RUSS 402 — Fourth Year Russian II may count toward any literature-and-culture category.

### South Slavic Studies — 18 hours beyond introductory culture

- Literature and culture: 6 hours, selected with the Undergraduate Advisor from the Slavic Department's maintained list.
- Upper-level literature and culture: 12 hours at the 300 or 400 level, selected with the Undergraduate Advisor from the department's maintained list.

Up to 9 hours of the literature requirement, including no more than 6 upper-level hours, may be replaced by same-level courses in other departments on the South Slavic region's history, culture, and society, with Undergraduate Advisor approval.

### Ukrainian Studies — 18 hours beyond introductory culture

- Literature surveys: 6 hours, selected with the Undergraduate Advisor from the Slavic Department's maintained list.
- Upper-level literature and culture: 12 hours at the 300 or 400 level, selected with the Undergraduate Advisor from the department's maintained list.

Up to 9 hours of the literature requirement, including no more than 6 upper-level hours, may be replaced by same-level courses in other departments on the Ukrainian region's history, culture, and society, with Undergraduate Advisor approval.
